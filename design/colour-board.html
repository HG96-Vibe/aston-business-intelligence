Home hero — colour directions

Same hero, same copy, same monogram and dot grid. Only the palette changes. The current site (first frame) shares Augustova's warm paper; the rest are different directions that keep gold as the signature. Each frame also shows the credentials strip and the start of the next section so you can see how the hero hands over.

Current — warm paper (Augustova)

For reference. Warm off-white, charcoal, gold. Calm and credible, but it's the same ground Augustova stands on.

A

Obsidian

Dark-first. Near-black ground, ivory type, gold at full brightness. The dot grid and traced monogram glow instead of whisper. Reads as luxury-tech. Day mode would become the secondary theme.

B

Racing green

Deep British racing green with gold — and it's called Aston. Unmistakably premium, unmistakably British, and nobody else in BI looks like this. The boldest option here, and the one with the clearest story.

C

Midnight navy

Navy and gold: private bank, wealth management, boardroom. The safest way to go dark — finance buyers already trust this pairing. Less distinctive than green, more conventional.

D

Gold glow

Obsidian with a soft gold light bleeding in from the top right behind the monogram. More atmospheric than A; the gold does the work of a hero image. Striking on a big screen.

E

Split

Keep the light ground for the copy, cut the right 40% to obsidian for the monogram and grid. Day and night in one frame — literally the toggle, as a composition. Distinctive without going fully dark.

F

Oxblood

Deep burgundy with gold. Old library, leather, claret. Very luxurious; a little heavier and warmer than green. Rare in B2B, which is the point.

G

Pure white & black

Drop the warmth: true white, true black, gold used sparingly. Crisper and more technical than paper — closer to a product company than a consultancy. The quietest way to separate from Augustova.

H

Champagne

Lean into gold as the ground itself: a pale champagne paper with deep bronze accents. Warm luxury — hotel, jeweller. Softest of the set; less "data", more "advisory".
